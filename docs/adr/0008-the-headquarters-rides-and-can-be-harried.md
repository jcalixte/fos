# The Headquarters rides, and can be harried off its ground

The **Headquarters** is no longer sited once and left there.

It **rides**: the player drags it during the battle and it walks to the ground he named at a
staff's pace, and no Courier leaves it while it is on the move. An enemy Unit within 300m
**harries** it, and every Order then waits a fixed twenty seconds at the table before its rider
sets off. An enemy Unit within 60m **overruns** it: the staff mounts and bolts three hundred
metres rearward, which is a ride, which is a blackout — and every Overrun leaves a permanent
surcharge on every Order for the rest of the afternoon.

This finishes [ADR-0002](./0002-orders-are-couriered-from-a-headquarters.md), which said the
Headquarters could be moved and shot at, and built neither.

## Why

**Sited once, it is not a decision the player makes — it is a fact about his Deployment.** ADR-0002
gives him exactly one: *where do I stand*, traded against safety and against sight. Asked before
the clock starts and never again, that trade is made blind, against an afternoon he has not seen
yet, and the delay he pays for the next forty minutes is the answer to a question he can no longer
revisit.

**But the ride and the danger are one feature, and shipping the ride alone would quietly undo F1.**
Today the Headquarters cannot be seen, cannot be shot at, cannot be reached, and Concealment — the
other half of ADR-0002's trade — is not built. A Headquarters that can move and cannot be touched
is a button that makes every Order faster: park it in the middle of the line, drag it forward as
the line advances, and the distance that was supposed to cost time stops costing any. The
Harried surcharge and the Overrun are what put the other side of the trade back on the Field.

**The blackout is not a second rule. It is the same fact as the ride.** A commander in the saddle
between two hills is not writing orders, and there is nobody standing where the last order came
from to write them either. So relocating four hundred metres costs a hundred seconds in which no
new Order can leave — which is what makes moving a commitment rather than a fidget, and is the
whole reason the pace can stay slow without needing a rule to stop the player chasing the action
with it.

**An Overrun has to be survivable, because the alternative hands the player a lost battle he still
has to sit through.** An army that cannot be ordered at all is a lose condition that does not end
the battle: ADR-0006 already spends the clock's full length on purpose, and twenty minutes of
watching with nothing to press is not a consequence, it is a punishment for having been
interested. A commander of the period who was ridden over did not silence his army — his staff got
up and rode, and everything afterwards was later and vaguer.

## Considered

**Capture, with the army going deaf.** The obvious shape, and it collapses the very trade it is
meant to sharpen. If standing forward can cost the whole battle, the risk side of *where do I
stand* is infinite and the question has one answer for every player in every scenario: the far
corner, all afternoon. A malus keeps it a curve; a silence makes it a cliff. It is also the shape
ADR-0002 refuses on its own terms — a silently discarded Order is unforgivable, and losing command
outright is that at full volume.

**The Headquarters as Key Ground.** Tempting, because possession is exactly what is at stake.
Refused on the definition: Key Ground is authored, fixed, and holdable by either army, and a
Headquarters is movable and only ever one army's. A piece of Key Ground that only one side can
hold and that walks away when threatened is not the same object, and reporting it in the Outcome
beside the bridge and the hamlet would misread the afternoon.

**A multiplier on the ride rather than a surcharge at the table** — slower riders while the staff
is harried. Better arithmetic: a flat term compresses the distance gradient the whole game rests
on, since twenty seconds is most of the ride to the reserve behind you and a sixth of the ride to
the flank. Rejected anyway, because it is *invisible*: a rider crossing the Field at ten metres a
second looks exactly like one crossing it at thirteen, and ADR-0002 draws the Courier precisely so
that delay is watched rather than felt as lag. A surcharge at the table can be drawn — the rider
waits at the Headquarters, in plain sight, and then gallops. The compression is the bill, and it
is paid in the right currency: at the moment the staff is being ridden at, the battalion two
hundred metres behind takes thirty-five seconds to hear about it.

**Sending the staff away by Order, like a Unit.** Rejected as a category error. The Headquarters is
where Orders come *from*; an Order it sent to itself would have to ride to itself, and the player
would be waiting on a Courier to tell him where he is standing.

## Consequence

**Three states, read off one number.** Standing, harried, riding — and the whole of it is how far
the nearest enemy Unit is, plus whether there is ground the staff is still trying to get to. A
Routing enemy harries nobody, on the same grounds that it holds no Key Ground: a mob streaming past
is in no position to threaten anything.

**The Overrun scar is the Morale Ceiling's shape, deliberately.** A Unit that has Rallied once
Breaks sooner; a Headquarters that has been ridden over once commands slower for the rest of the
day, and again if it happens twice. One familiar pattern, no new concept, and it means an Overrun
is felt long after the horsemen have gone.

**The enemy pays nothing for any of this yet, and the rule is written over both armies anyway.**
The Plan applies its Orders where they land rather than couriering them, so an enemy Headquarters
that is harried or overrun loses exactly nothing — and only the player's own is drawn, because
there is no point offering him a target that cannot be hurt. Written symmetrically so it lights up
the day the enemy is couriered; until then the friction is the commanding side's, which is the
same asymmetry the enemy already enjoys everywhere else.

**The staff pulls up at impassable Ground.** It rides straight and does not route: no A\*, no
Crossings, no funnel. What it will not do is ford a river — a ride that would step onto impassable
Ground settles where it stands instead, which reads as the staff pulling up at the bank and needs
no rule of its own.

**It is now possible to be out of command at the worst possible moment, by choice.** Ordering the
ride during a crisis is a bad idea, and nothing stops the player. That is the decision.
